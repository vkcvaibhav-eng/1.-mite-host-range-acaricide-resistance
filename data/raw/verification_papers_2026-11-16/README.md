# Priority case verification: 21 cases

Checked 15 November 2026. The updated verification workbook is supplied separately.
All 21 requested rows have entries: 15 supported and 6 partly verified. Missing full text has not been labelled "not traceable".

## PDFs in this package

The original_studies folder contains three complete original studies:

- Pan et al. (2020), Journal of Economic Entomology 113:918-923. Published article. G20600: Table 1, p.919; Table 2, p.920. Anyue fenpropathrin 52.27-fold in 2017 and 47.27-fold in 2018.
- Rameshgar et al. (2019), Crop Protection 125:104903. Accepted manuscript, not publisher typesetting. G20230: Table 2, PDF p.20. Mahabad abamectin 46.13-fold as reported.
- Sato et al. (2004), Applied Entomology and Zoology 39:293-302. Published article. G10191: Table 3, p.298. Pyridaben RR50=53.1 after laboratory selection, not a field-population ratio.

The citing_references folder contains three complete supporting works. These are not the unavailable originals they cite:

- Khajehali (2010), Biochemical and molecular mechanisms of acaricide resistance in Tetranychus urticae. Ghent University PhD thesis, 207 PDF pages. G2721: printed p.23 (PDF p.39), with the Overmeer, Van Zon & Helle (1975) bibliography entry on printed p.155 (PDF p.171). Supports dicofol resistance in Aalsmeer, Netherlands.
- De Beer et al. (2022), QTL mapping suggests that both cytochrome P450-mediated detoxification and target-site resistance are involved in fenbutatin oxide resistance in Tetranychus urticae. DOI:10.1016/j.ibmb.2022.103757. Journal pre-proof. G7283/G7287: Methods section 2.1, printed p.4 (PDF p.6), lines 118-120, confirms the MR-VL greenhouse origin at Ghent University and cites Van Leeuwen et al. (2005) for bifenthrin/dicofol resistance. The 2022 selection results are not assigned as 2005 ratios.
- Yaman, Yorulmaz Salman & Ay (2016), Journal of Agricultural Sciences 22:249-260. DOI:10.1501/Tarimbil_0000001385. Published Turkish-language paper. G14668: p.259 (PDF p.11), cites Auger et al. (2003) and reports tebufenpyrad 16.8-39.8-fold resistance. The filename uses the printed publication year 2016, not the date the online file was indexed.

## Cases requiring recheck

- G20360: cyflumetofen resistance is demonstrated in laboratory-selected strains; field ancestry does not by itself establish field-evolved resistance before selection.
- G19407: Brazilian field monitoring is supported, but the Artur Nogueira-specific row was not recovered. The 121-fold ratio is from laboratory selection; 0-81.5% is a resistance frequency range, not a fold range.
- G10191: 53.1-fold pyridaben resistance is a laboratory cross-resistance result after fenpyroximate selection; the unselected field pyridaben ratio is not established.
- G7174, G7194, G7205: Suh et al. (2006) original text and numbered tables were examined online. Mapping Gumi, Cheongju and Yeongcheon to the numbered populations remains unresolved, so ratios from other table rows were not substituted.
